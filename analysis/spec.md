# Original macro-panel analysis: questions the literature cannot yet answer (specified 3 October 2026, before data were downloaded)

The review (`review/`) could not answer four questions because the included studies do not contain the evidence: (1) the shape of the effect by adoption level, (2) effects by income group under a common design, (3) timing around technology arrival, and (4) differences inside low-income economies. This analysis uses public World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI) data to give a first, transparent answer to each. A fifth question, cost-effectiveness, cannot be answered because WDI has no investment cost, price or subsidy series for these technologies; it stays open.

**Status of the evidence.** Country-level fixed-effects panels give conditional associations. They do not identify causal effects: adoption is not randomly assigned, reverse causation is likely, and no instrument is available here. Every result below is exploratory and written as an association. Income groups use the World Bank's current classification (a time-invariant label); a sensitivity check uses initial GDP per capita.

## Data
- World Bank WDI via the public API (bulk by indicator, 1995 to 2023), economies only (aggregates removed). Income group and region from the World Bank country list.
- Technology: mobile cellular subscriptions per 100 people (IT.CEL.SETS.P2, main), individuals using the internet, % of population (IT.NET.USER.ZS), fixed broadband subscriptions per 100 (IT.NET.BBND.P2).
- Outcomes: GDP per capita, constant 2015 US$ (NY.GDP.PCAP.KD) as the main outcome; secondary outcomes: life expectancy (SP.DYN.LE00.IN), under-5 mortality (SH.DYN.MORT), unemployment (SL.UEM.TOTL.ZS), agriculture share of value added (NV.AGR.TOTL.ZS), CO2 emissions per capita (EN.GHG.CO2.PC.CE.AR5 if available, else EN.ATM.CO2E.PC), electric power consumption per capita (EG.USE.ELEC.KH.PC).
- Controls and moderators: gross secondary enrolment (SE.SEC.ENRR), trade % of GDP (NE.TRD.GNFS.ZS), domestic credit to the private sector % of GDP (FS.AST.PRVT.GD.ZS), access to electricity % of population (EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS).
- Main period 1995 to 2019 (avoids the pandemic); sensitivity to 1995 to 2023.

## Analyses (all pre-specified; every one is reported)
**A1. Income-group effects under one design (question 2).** Non-overlapping 5-year periods (1995 to 1999, ..., 2015 to 2019). Outcome: average annual growth of GDP per capita in the period (100 times log difference divided by years). Regressor: change in the technology measure over the same period (per 10 units). Controls: initial log GDP per capita, initial secondary enrolment, change in trade openness. Period fixed effects. Country fixed effects are absorbed because the outcome is a growth rate; a second version adds country fixed effects explicitly. The coefficient is estimated separately for the four income groups by interaction, standard errors clustered by country.
**A2. Predetermined version.** As A1 but the regressor is the change in the technology measure in the previous period (the outcome period is the next one), controlling for lagged growth. This reduces simultaneity but does not remove reverse causation.
**A3. Dose-response (question 1).** As A1 with the change in adoption entered separately for four baseline adoption bins (level at start of period: below 10, 10 to 40, 40 to 80, above 80 per 100 for mobile). Pooled across income groups, then separately for low and lower-middle income against upper-middle and high.
**A4. Event study around arrival (question 3).** Takeoff year = first year the mobile measure reaches 10 per 100 people, among economies below 10 in 1995 (so the pre-period is observed). Stacked cohort design: for each cohort, treated economies with that takeoff year and clean controls (takeoff more than 10 years later or never by 2023). Event window from 5 years before to 10 years after, reference year -1, cohort-by-country and cohort-by-year fixed effects, standard errors clustered by country. Outcome: log GDP per capita times 100. Estimated pooled and separately for low plus lower-middle income, upper-middle income and high income. Pre-trends are reported.
**A5. Beyond income (question 3, secondary).** The A4 design applied to the secondary outcomes, reporting the effect 5 and 10 years after takeoff, by income group where sample size allows.
**A6. Differences inside low and lower-middle income economies (question 4).** Within the 5-year-period panel of low and lower-middle income economies only, interact the change in adoption (A1) with initial secondary enrolment, electricity access, private credit and initial log GDP per capita, one moderator at a time, each standardised. Report coefficients and p-values.

## Decision rules
- Two-sided tests; effects are described by estimate and confidence interval, not by significance alone. No multiplicity correction; roughly 30 estimates are reported, so about three nominal p-values below 0.10 are expected by chance.
- Samples with fewer than 15 economies or fewer than 30 observations in a cell are not interpreted.
- No result is dropped. Departures from this plan are listed in the results file.
