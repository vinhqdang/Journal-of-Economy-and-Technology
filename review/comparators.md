# Existing meta-analyses used as comparators (read in full by the reviewer, 19 November 2026)

- **Stanley, Doucouliagos and Steel (2018), Journal of Economic Surveys 32(3), 705-726.** Meta-regression of 466 estimates from 59 econometric studies of ICT and growth. Overall weighted-average partial correlation about 0.2 (Table 1: 0.195 fixed effects, 0.245 random effects, 0.195 WLS, 415 estimates, 58 studies); publication-selection bias detected (FAT slope 1.92); bias-corrected effects positive and about 0.2 (PEESE 0.175, cluster-robust CI 0.098 to 0.251; PET 0.109 WLS); "small" effect (about 4% of residual variance); mobile effect about twice landline; developed countries gain more from computers than developing countries; little evidence of an internet effect.
- **Foster, Gorgulu, Jain, Straub and Vagliasindi (2026), World Bank Research Observer 41(2).** Meta-analysis of over a thousand estimates from 221 papers on infrastructure (transport, energy, digital/ICT) and development outcomes. Digital elasticity in the range 0.018 to 0.021 after accounting for publication bias; effects larger in developed countries for digital and energy studies, larger for transport and cross-sectoral studies in developing countries.
- **Oloyede et al. (2023), Heliyon 9, e17654.** Systematic literature review of the definition and measurement of the digital economy in developing countries; it reports no pooled effect size and is not a comparator for the pooled partial correlation. It supports the point that the digital economy is defined and measured differently across studies.
